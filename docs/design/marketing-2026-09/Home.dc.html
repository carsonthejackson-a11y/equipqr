How it works

From the machine to resolved, in three steps.

The same flow whether the person scanning is your customer, a line cook at 7:40 on a Friday, or a technician on your own crew.

01

Scan the tag

A durable QR sticker goes on the unit when you set it up. Scanning it opens a page for that exact machine, on any phone: make, model, location, who services it, and what to do next. No app to install, no account to make.

Print codes per unit as SVG or PNG, or order pre-printed batches and claim each sticker on-site by photographing the nameplate.

Work the guide

A short branching guide covers the usual causes first: breaker, water supply, drain screen, filter. Each step is a question, with a photo where it helps. A chat assistant answers anything the steps didn't cover.

Questions

The things people ask before their first sticker.

More on the full FAQ, including billing and restaurant-specific answers.

Do customers or staff need an app or an account?

No. Scanning the sticker opens a mobile web page: no app store, no login, no account to create, no email address required. If the guide doesn't fix it, they submit the request from that same page.

How do I build the troubleshooting guides?

Describe the equipment type and its common failure modes once, and EquipQR drafts a full branching guide in minutes. Review it, edit any step, and publish. Every unit of that type uses it from then on.

What if a sticker hasn't been assigned to a unit yet?

Customers see a plain "this isn't set up yet, contact the service company" message, with no dead links. A signed-in technician who scans it can claim it to a piece of equipment on the spot, photographing the nameplate to fill in the details.

What happens if a vendor doesn't respond?

You get an email as soon as the vendor's typical response window passes, with their phone number in it. EquipQR sends the work order and shows every status change, but a phone call is the reliable check when it's urgent.

Can I control what my team can see and do?

Yes. Owners manage billing, the team, and company settings. Technicians manage equipment, customers, and requests but can't touch billing or remove the company. Every account belongs to exactly one company, and data never crosses between companies.

Built on a real route, not a whiteboard.
